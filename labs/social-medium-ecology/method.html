About

What this guide is, and is not

Social Medium Ecology applies the tradition of media ecology and its critics to the large social platforms of 2026. Its claim is modest: these frameworks, applied consistently, make the platforms easier to compare and easier to choose between. It is not a ranking of platform quality, and it is not a validated psychometric instrument.

Three kinds of content appear throughout, and the site labels them:

  • Sourced facts: user figures, product events and dates, each linked to a source.
  • Attributed arguments: paraphrases of what a thinker argued, cited to a specific work. Quotations are kept short.
  • Interpretation: the trait codings, chart placements, tetrads and Doctorow stages. These are this guide's readings and are published in full below so they can be contested.

Several of the thinkers died before smartphones existed. Applying McLuhan, Innis, Williams, Illich, Ong or Postman to a recommendation feed is an extrapolation by this guide, not a claim they made.

Trait coding

How the platforms are coded

Each platform is coded from 0 to 4 on eight structural traits and six formats. Codes reflect each platform's default experience for a typical user in 2026, based on its documented features, business model and policies. They are judgments, not measurements; half-points mark close calls.

Four of the six Atlas lenses are computed directly from these codes, so the charts and the self-assessment use one consistent description of each platform. McLuhan's hot/cool lens is placed by hand, and the clusters lens groups by dominant form.

Scoring

How the self-assessments score

Values mode. Each answer states a preferred position (0–4) on one or more traits, with a weight reflecting how strongly that answer expresses a preference. Answers that express indifference carry no weight. Preferences on the same trait are averaged by weight. A platform's alignment is the weighted average closeness between your preferred positions and its codes:

alignment = Σ wᵢ (1 − |preferredᵢ − codeᵢ| / 4) / Σ wᵢ

Formats are handled separately: a platform scores on its strongest match among the formats you chose, so choosing several formats never penalizes a platform for missing one. The platforms you use weekly are not scored; they are compared with your results afterwards.

Presence mode. This mode is practical rather than theoretical. Each answer adds or subtracts points for specific platforms based on fit with the goal, audience, format, capacity, risk tolerance and topic you describe. Your existing audience counts most (3 points per platform), and enjoyment adds 2, because a presence you dislike maintaining tends not to last. Platforms you exclude are removed from the tiers, and the results note what you give up if one would have ranked in the top four.

Clusters. Each cluster's share reflects its best-scoring platform, sharpened so that clear differences show up as clear shares.

Limits. Weights and codes have not been validated against outcomes. Treat the results as a structured prompt for your own judgment.

The drift chart

Estimating the move from chosen to ranked feeds

Each platform gets an estimate, from 0 to 100, of how much of its default feed is selected by a ranking model rather than by the user's follows or chronology. Estimates are anchored to dated product changes and interpolated between them. The anchoring events are:

    Doctorow stages

    Placing platforms on the curve

    Cory Doctorow's model has three stages (Doctorow 2023; Doctorow 2025). Each platform's position, from 0 to 3, is a reading of public changes to its reach, pricing, ad load and terms. The reasoning for every placement appears in the Atlas table and in the platform profiles.

    User figures

    How many people use each platform

    Companies define "active user" differently, many no longer publish monthly figures, and some report different metrics. Each figure is labeled by kind: company-reported (earnings releases and filings), company claim (statements by executives, not audited), third-party estimate, or a different metric used as a stand-in. Figures are as of October 2026.
