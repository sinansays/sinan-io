The Atlas

Seventeen platforms read through the media ecologists, the political economists and their optimist critics. One arc runs through all of it. Social media began as a map of people you chose. It became a feed chosen for you. Now social life is splitting into private rooms and open protocols, while the feeds keep growing.

Social graph→ Algorithmic feed→ Retreat and federation

Placements on these charts are interpretive codings, not measurements. Each one is listed in the coding table, with the reasoning.

Five media, not seventeen apps

The clusters

McLuhan's starting point was that the form of a medium matters more than what is said on it. Grouped by form, the seventeen platforms reduce to five kinds of medium. Each section pairs the thinkers who cut deepest there with a moving diagram of the network's shape.

Plate 3

McLuhan's tetrads

In Laws of Media (1988), Marshall and Eric McLuhan proposed four questions for any medium. What does it enhance? What does it make obsolete? What older thing does it bring back? What does it flip into when pushed to its limit? Pick a platform.

Plate 4

Where each platform sits on Doctorow's curve

Cory Doctorow's model has three stages. A platform is first good to its users. Then it squeezes users to serve its business customers. Then it squeezes both for shareholders, until it dies or is replaced. The interesting cases refuse the curve by design: a nonprofit (Signal) and protocols that let you leave with your followers (Mastodon, and in principle Bluesky).
